## Facts by chapter

### 1. The Divide
- A glacier about 12,000 years ago left a continental divide just south of
  town; water to the north drains to Lake Erie, to the south to the Ohio River. (CAN, WIKI)
- The site was at the edge of the Great Black Swamp. (SET)
- The Auglaize Trail was a Native trading path and portage between the Miami and
  St. Marys rivers; CAN calls it "that oft-used portage across the divide along the old
  Auglaize Trail and near the site of the future New Bremen", hence "crossed the divide
  nearby". (CAN)

### 2. Bremen in the Clearing
- 1832: a company of 33 members formed in Cincinnati. (SET, WIKI)
- Scouts F. H. Schroeder and A. F. Windeler searched much of Ohio and part of
  Indiana, choosing a partly cleared site at the edge of the swamp. (SET)
- Windeler brought six families on a two-week, 120-mile trek; all lived at
  first in Schroeder's 12 x 14 ft log hut. (SET)
- June 11, 1833: the plat of "Bremen" recorded at St. Marys; 102 lots, one per
  member, the rest sold for $25. (SET, CHR, CAN)
- 1835: post office opens and the name becomes New Bremen. (CHR, SET, CAN)
- Image: NBHA photos1083 is a drawing, not a photograph, so its credit and alt text say
  "Drawing" (`kind = "drawing"` in photos.toml). NBHA's caption says "begun in 1837"; NBHA
  says "believed built about 1837", so the credit says "built about 1837".

### 3. The Canal Comes Through
- The canal reached New Bremen in 1845; the whole canal ran about 250 miles,
  Cincinnati to Toledo. (CAN)
- Travel time dropped from weeks over land to days. (CAN says 5 weeks to 5 days.)
- Boats 70–80 ft long, pulled by three mules in tandem. (CAN)
- Locks 90 ft long and 15 ft wide. (CAN)
- Lock One is in town; photographed "looking North at Lock I" in 1904. (HOME slider, CAN)

### 4. A Canal Boomtown
- 1850: 5 hotels, 10 saloons, 6 dance halls, 10 cooperages, 6 blacksmiths. (CHR, CAN)
- Hog shipping peaked above 10,000 hogs by canal boat. (SET)
- Charles Boesel Sr. opened the first general store and was a leader in the
  grain and pork trade. (SET)

### 5. Iron Rails
- Railroads began drawing trade from the canal in the late 1850s; the canal
  carried the iron for the rails. (CAN)
- A rail line came through New Bremen in 1871. (SET)

### 6. Frogtown and Cheesequarters
- Bremen was west of the canal; Ober Bremen, platted 1853, east of it. (CHR, CAN)
- Nicknames: "Frogtown" (east), "Cheesequarters" (west). (CHR, CAN)
- Vote of 101 to 16 on October 12, 1875; annexation in 1876. (CHR, CAN)

### 7. Gaslight and an Opera House
- Natural gas lit on July 3, 1888, before crowds. (CHR, SET)
- Electric light franchise 1893, telephone lines 1897, municipal water works and
  electric plant 1902. (CHR)
- Boesel Opera House built for $6,925; opened January 3, 1896. (CHR, GAL)

### 8. The Interurban
- Western Ohio Railway: first car April 21, 1902. (SET)
- Cars ran on Main Street. (GAL caption, Wietholter print subject)
- Abandoned 1931. (CHR, SET) "Until cars and paved roads took over" is general context for
  why interurbans closed, not from a cited source. An NBHA gallery caption says it ran "from 1902 to 1932"; the
  copy follows the centennial book.

### 9. The Speedway
- **PROG**: Official 1968 New Bremen Speedway Program, in the NBHA speedway gallery.
  https://newbremenhistory.org/en/content/111-new-bremen-speedway
- Racing at the New Bremen Speedway in the early 1930s. (GAL captions: "At the New Bremen
  Speedway. Early 1930's racing.", "John Wissman in race car in 1930.")
- Covered grandstand and dirt track in the 1930s. (NBHA photo 862)
- A half-mile asphalt oval by 1967; USAC (United States Auto Club) sprint races; three USAC
  sprint events in 1968. (PROG p. 4)
- Track record: Larry Dickson, 18.42 seconds, August 27, 1967, in the USAC twin 50s, "almost 98
  miles an hour". (PROG p. 4)
- Racing ended in the 1970s. (GAL caption: "Last days of racing in the 1970's.")
- Left out: the 1931 riot and fire.

### 10. The Centennial
- Centennial celebration July 1–4, 1933. (CHR) "In the middle of the Depression" is the
  date's general context.
- The centennial book's histories are by C. A. Schrage and W. W. Grothaus. (CHR, SET)

### 11. Crown
- **WIKI-C**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Equipment_Corporation (Crown's own
  history pages returned 404 when checked, 2026-11-05).
- **CROWN**: https://www.crown.com/en-us/about-us.html (via search summary).
- The company made temperature controls for coal-burning furnaces in the 1920s as the
  Pioneer Heat Regulator Company; in 1945 it became Crown Controls. (WIKI-C)
- Founders: WIKI-C names Carl and Allen Dicke and Carl's son Jim; CROWN credits brothers
  Carl H. and Allen A. Dicke. The copy says "the Dicke family".
- TV antenna rotators from 1949 (made until 2000). (WIKI-C)
- First lift truck: 1956 per WIKI-C, 1957 (the LT-500) per encyclopedia.com. The copy says
  "the late 1950s".
- About 20,900 employees; privately held; Jim Dicke II CEO and Jim Dicke III president;
  headquarters in New Bremen; fifth-largest lift truck maker (Modern Materials Handling,
  2022). (WIKI-C) The copy says "one of the largest" and "more than 20,000".
- Photo: NBHA photos165, "Crown former antenna rotor plant in the 1970's, formerly Rabe
  Hardware Store."

### 12. Saving the Story
- **THOMPSON**: https://newbremenhistory.org/en/content/20-the-thompson-house (citing
  The Evening Leader, 1968): the livery stable was razed from Feb 25, 1968; the lock
  tender's house, which "also served as a hotel for the boatmen", was burned in April 1968;
  the land went to the village for a Lions Club park. The copy says only that they "came
  down ... to make room for a park"; the burning (a fire-training exercise) is left out.
- **NBHA**: https://newbremenhistory.org/en/content/77-new-bremen-historic-association
  - Backyard gathering "on the evening of Thursday, September 27, 1973", discussing "the
    sad loss of the Thompson Hotel and Livery at Lock 1".
  - October 3, 1973: motion passed to form the association ("within a week").
  - The Luelleman House: "one of the first non-log-cabin structures in the original plat
    of 1833", believed built about 1837; bought with pledges (initial price $15,500). The
    copy says "one of the first houses in town not built of logs".
  - Museum dedicated July 4, 1976, "as the United States celebrated its 200th birthday",
    "completely paid for by pledges".
  - Don't repeat the page's "35th anniversary of this dedication" line (2008); the
    arithmetic matches the founding, not the dedication.
- Photo: NBHA museums gallery, Luelleman House, 2024.

### 13. Bremenfest
- First year: the village says "Bremenfest began in 1974"
  (https://newbremen.com/festivals/); the NBHA says 1975 (home page and Bremenfest
  gallery). The 2026 schedule calls it the "51st Annual Bremenfest Parade". The copy says
  "the mid-1970s".
- Held every August ("third weekend of August", village); 2026 ran Aug 13-16, four days.
  (https://bremenfest.com/schedule-2/)
- Parade, food tents, live bands, pageants (Little Miss and Miss Bremenfest), car show,
  5K, bed races, duck races. (village page, 2026 schedule) Duck race on the canal, 2004.
  (GAL 2001-present caption)
- Photo: NBHA Bremenfest gallery, slide 015 (uncaptioned). A sign on the float reads
  "1975 Bremenfest Queen"; the banner reads "Bremenfest, August 25-26-27, New Bremen". The
  festival year isn't stated, so the credit only describes what's shown.

### 14. The Story Continues
- Lock One restoration and canal cleaning, June 2006. (GAL 2001-present captions)
- Towpath trail. (CAN, WIKI)
- Population 3,034 in 2020. (WIKI)
- Crown Equipment world headquarters; Bicycle Museum of America. (WIKI)
- NBHA museum on North Main Street. (NBHA)
- **PAV**: https://newbremenhistory.org/en/content/126-the-crown-pavilion (quoting HA
  Dorsten): the pavilion was built around 1900 on Orchard Island, Indian Lake; the James F.
  Dicke family bought the property around 2000; the project was approved by July 2003 and
  the steel structure re-erected in New Bremen. The copy says "built around 1900 on an
  island in Indian Lake" and "moved here by the Dicke family in the early 2000s".
- Photo: NBHA scenes 2019 93 (uncaptioned aerial). The round building beside the canal is
  the Crown Pavilion: its green roof and red steel posts match the photo in PAV.

## Left out on purpose

- **1849 cholera in Amsterdam, 1913 flood, 1897 St. Paul's fire, 1909 interurban
  accident:** dropped to keep the story upbeat.
- **Treaty of Greenville (1795):** NBHA says it opened the area, but the treaty
  line ran near Fort Loramie and this land may have been ceded later (1818,
  Treaty of St. Mary's). Not used until verified.
- **Land purchase size:** SET and CHR say 10 acres at $1 an acre; CAN and WIKI
  say 80 acres. Not used.
- **Lot lottery:** only in WIKI. Not used.
- **Rail line name:** SET names it; not repeated in the copy until checked.
